# Townsfolk

A top-down pixel-art civilization sim where the townsfolk build the town themselves. You never place a building: you watch, set the town's priorities, and throw world events at it.

Eighteen founders start in a small town (an office, a café, a market, a library, six homes, a farm and a storehouse) surrounded by forest, rock and a lake. From there they hold town meetings, decide what the town needs, pick sites, clear trees, haul timber and stone, and build homes, farms, a school, diners, parks and storehouses. People court, marry in front of family and friends, move in together, have children who inherit their traits, grow up into jobs, retire, die and are buried with a funeral; newcomers arrive along the east road when there's room. Each person has needs, traits, skills, a schedule, memories and opinions of everyone else, and decides what to do next with a utility AI.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test           # sim + pathfinding tests
npm run build      # typecheck + production bundle
```

`?seed=42` in the URL gives a different cast.

## How the town builds itself

- **Town meetings** (`src/sim/planner.ts`): every couple of hours the town weighs its needs: homeless families, couples who want a place of their own, overcrowded homes, food production against appetite, kids without a school, crowded cafés, the lack of green space. The best proposal is put forward by the person it matters most to ("Nia proposed a new home because Nia and Dara want a home of their own").
- **Sites**: the planner scans for open land near town, prefers fewer trees to clear, and routes a dirt path from the new door to the nearest street.
- **Construction** (`src/sim/labor.ts`): builders and laborers clear the site (the timber and stone go straight into the build), haul the rest from the storehouse, then build. Walls rise and scaffolding goes up as work progresses.
- **Economy**: woodcutters keep the stockpile topped up from the forest (which slowly regrows); farmers harvest and tend crops; food flows from farms to the storehouse to the market, kitchens and diners; households cook from their own pantry.
- **Growing homes and town tiers** (`src/sim/growth.ts`): a family with young kids outgrowing its house builds a wing instead of moving: the side wall comes down and the home gets four tiles wider with two more beds (the founders' houses are packed in a row, so it's the town's own newer houses that grow). Grown kids and new couples look for land near their parents. At 30 people and 18 buildings the village becomes a town, and at 50 and 30 a city, each with a charter day festival; a town paves its dirt paths and trodden trails in stone, spreading out from the streets, and can take on more projects at once. The Town view shows the tier and what the next one needs.
- **Elections** (`src/sim/politics.ts`): on the first day of every summer up to three well-liked, persuasive adults stand for mayor (a sitting mayor stands again), each on what matters most to them that nobody else is running on: homes, food, wood & stone, or community. They make their case at a rally in the park at 17:00 ("vote Ula: more homes!"), and at 19:00 every adult votes for the candidate they like best, weighed by whether the platform speaks to their own situation and by family ties. The mayor chairs town meetings and their agenda nudges the planner (×1.25), on top of your priorities, which stay in charge. The Town view shows the mayor, the race and the last vote.
- **Workshop** (`src/sim/workshop.ts`): once the town reaches about 22 people with timber to spare, a town meeting proposes a workshop. Its trades work three kinds of station: a carpenter saws wood into planks and builds furniture at the bench, a mason fires stone into bricks in the kiln, and a toolmaker makes tools from planks and stone. After that, homes, schools, diners and storehouses are built from planks and bricks, and new homes need two pieces of furniture before anyone moves in. Workers pick up fresh tools from the stores and work 25% faster until they wear through. The Town view lists each chain, how stocked it is, and what's holding it up ("Stuck: needs wood", "no mason").
- **Money** (`src/sim/market.ts`): the town's shops are run by the town, so groceries, café meals and coffee pay into the treasury, and the office pays 15% tax on what its outside clients pay. Out of the treasury come town wages (builders, farmers, crafters, shop staff, teachers) and the coins each new building costs; when the treasury can't cover a project it waits (homes for the homeless are built anyway). Prices follow stock: food gets dear when the stores run low and cheap in a glut. Every three days a trader's cart pulls up at the market to buy surplus and sell food, firewood or tools the town is short of. Retirees draw a small pension. The treasury keeps a few days of payroll in reserve and pays out part of anything above it each midnight as bonuses to town workers, weighted to whoever has least, and tops up any town worker down to their last few dollars. Short of money for groceries, family chip in (nobody walks to the market if they can't buy anything); a household broke and miserable for three days in a row packs up and leaves.
- **Jobs**: flexible roles (builder, farmer, woodcutter, laborer, carpenter, mason, toolmaker, teacher, cook) are reassigned as the town's needs change. Office, café, market and library staff keep their jobs.
- **Generations** (`src/sim/life.ts`): two years pass per game day. Kids are born to couples with a spare bed, go to school once there is one, and come of age at 18; people retire at 66; grief spreads to family and friends.
- **Health** (`src/sim/health.ts`): colds and flu pass between people who chat, share a table or share a home, more in winter and when they're cold; storms and work accidents cause injuries. The ill call in sick and rest in bed, at home or at the clinic, and family look in on them; rest, care, a doctor and medicine all speed recovery, and recovering leaves a few days' immunity. Frailty builds from about 62 and with every illness, and frailty plus serious illness is what ends a life; the family visits more as someone grows frail. After an outbreak (also a world event) or with several people ill, a town meeting builds a clinic; its doctor treats patients and mixes medicine from herbs the farmers bring in.
- **School and careers** (`src/sim/careers.ts`): kids are born with leanings from their traits and their parents' trades, and build four aptitudes (hands-on, numbers, people, outdoors) in class (more with a teacher at the board) and playing outside. At 18 they pick a trade by aptitude, what their parents do, the adult they most look up to, and what the town has nobody doing (office jobs need a free desk; the trades need a workshop). They apprentice for two days under a mentor, a parent if possible, learning twice as fast and three times as fast at the mentor's side. Job allocation puts people who trained for a trade into it first. Skill and tools change real output: a master works much faster than a novice.
- **Family life**: once a calendar year, on the day they were born, people throw a birthday party at home at 18:00: cake on the table, family comes over (friends too for round numbers like 18, 50 or 80). Households sit down to dinner together. Babies are sometimes named after a grandparent who has passed away.
- **Out and about**: footsteps wear grass into trampled patches and then bare trails along the routes people actually take (and trails are quicker to walk, so they stick); trails nobody uses grass over in a few days. After dinner, couples go for a walk side by side, and on school mornings a grown-up at home walks the little ones to school. Most people carry an umbrella in the rain; the rest hurry and get soaked.
- **Seasons** (`src/sim/seasons.ts`): spring and summer grow crops (summer fastest), autumn slows them and turns the trees copper, and the first frost of winter clears whatever is still in the fields, ripe or not; nothing grows until spring, so the town lives on what it harvested in time, and the cold makes everyone hungrier. A Low food priority staffs fewer farmers (and no laborers in the fields), so less of the autumn harvest gets in, and nobody starts a new farm in winter. From late autumn people get cold outdoors (a Warmth need) and warm up at home by the hearth, which burns firewood: woodcutters, laborers and idle winter farmers split logs at the storehouse and carry firewood to the homes running lowest. Autumn brings storms and winter the odd blizzard; each season has a festival in the park (Spring fair, Midsummer night, Harvest festival, Winter lights with a bonfire). The Town view shows how many days of food and firewood are left as winter approaches.
- **Cemetery**: the first death prompts a town meeting to build one; each death adds a named headstone, and family visit the grave now and then, which eases their grief.
- **The lake** (`src/sim/lake.ts`): once the town has a dozen-plus mouths to feed, a town meeting proposes a fishing dock on the lake shore ("fish don't stop at the first frost"): a sandy lot with a boardwalk and a pier two boards wide running out over the water, a pail and rod at each of the two places at the end and at more along the sides of the pier wherever open water lies beside it. Fishers stand there for 40-minute stints and carry the catch to the storehouse: 2.5 food a stint in spring and summer, 3 in autumn, 1.5 through the ice in winter, more with practice, which makes it the one harvest that outlasts the first frost. Fishers are sized by the town: one before autumn only if the town eats more than it grows or the stores are thin, then from autumn a fisher for every 14 mouths (every 10 through the ice), never fewer than two and never more than the pier has places. They're staffed after the farm crew in spring and summer; from autumn the first goes ahead of the farm crew and the rest ahead of the builders, and in winter all go ahead of the farm crew, whose fields are bare. The food lever adds or removes one. The dock is a rush job from late summer, ahead of new homes and allowed past the usual one-project limit, so it stands before the first frost. In winter the lake (and the park pond) freezes and can be walked on: people go skating on free afternoons, and when the ice breaks up in spring anyone out on it steps ashore. On summer afternoons people sit by the water.
- **Fire** (`src/sim/fire.ts`): rare (a town sees one every few in-game years), likelier in summer and droughts, from a winter hearth, a workshop kiln, or lightning in a storm, and rolled on dice of its own so a town's story only changes once something actually catches. A fire grows, chars the building (its walls and roof darken with soot) and, once fierce, can throw sparks to a building within three tiles. The alarm goes up every two minutes while it burns (the whole town is called every tenth): grown-ups awake within 60 tiles come running with buckets and stand round it ("water!"), sleepers wake from 25 tiles or when a neighbour who's up shouts, and the line has seven places, so a crowd doesn't help. Children, elders and the ill aren't called out of bed, only turned out of the burning building, and nobody walks into one for any other reason. Each firefighter beats it back (more with athletics, with a well within 25 tiles, or in the rain), so a full daytime crew with a well saves the building in half an hour with a scorch you can see (5-20% damage; without a well it takes an hour and a half), a drought fire is harder (a gutted home without a well), and a night fire with two or three people gets away from them and burns the house out. Scorches under 5% are left to fade; nobody goes out to repair them. Buildings are never lost outright: a fire nobody fights burns out and leaves a shell. Food and firewood in a burned home burn with it; a home past 60% damage can't be slept in, and a gutted workshop can't be worked, until builders repair it (wood and work, gutted homes ahead of any new build). Planks-and-bricks buildings burn slower. After the town's first fire, meetings make wells a priority (one per eight homes), ahead of new homes and past the usual one-project limit. Flames and a glow over the roof show above the night grade; the director cuts to a fire before anything else; Fire is also a world event in the Town panel.
- **Travelers between towns** (`src/sim/travel.ts`): every town has a name now (Northwind until you rename it from the Town panel). **Journey** on someone's card sends them, or their whole household, to another town: they leave for good (close friends and family miss them; nobody grieves), kids left behind are taken in, and you get a small `traveler-*.json` file. Anyone can **Welcome travelers…** from the Town panel with that file: a day and a half later a cart comes down the east road ("Milo Okafor, a software engineer from Ashford, with Ula and Mateo"), and they arrive with their ages, looks, traits, skills, trade, leanings, an unfinished dream, their big memories (labeled "back home" unless about each other), family ties within the party, and a purse of up to $200. The file is checked field by field: at most 8 people, names and labels stripped of markup, unknown traits and memories dropped, numbers clamped, a journey can't arrive twice, and a town expects at most two carts a day. A traveler from a town with your town's name comes "from another town called Northwind"; a name that's taken here gets a numeral.
- **The chronicle** (`src/sim/chronicle.ts`, `src/ui/chronicle.ts`; `H`, or "Read the chronicle" in the Town view): every headline the town has had is kept for good (the feed only holds its last 500 lines), along with the town's size on the first morning of each year. From those it writes a page per year, newest first: "Northwind grew from 18 to 25 people, with one birth, one wedding and 6 new buildings", then the headlines by season, a closing line when the year earned one ("More cradles than graves."), and a byline from the office writer if there is one ("Kept by Wim Lindqvist, writer"). Families shows In memoriam (an obituary for everyone who has died, built only from what's true of their life and who they left behind) and a family tree for anyone, living, dead or moved away, three generations each way; names link through, and the living open in the inspector. Clicking a headstone in the cemetery opens that person's page. Older saves start their chronicle from the headlines still in their feed.
- **Life aspirations** (`src/sim/aspirations.ts`): every grown-up carries one dream that fits them: master their trade, be elected mayor, raise a big family, a home of their own, find someone to share life with, a true friend, see Northwind become a city, dance at four festivals in a row, help raise five buildings, or (the competitive, with a rival) outlive that rival. The inspector shows it under their traits with how close they are ("Dreams of mastering engineering · level 5 of 8"). Dreams lean gently on what people choose (a would-be master puts in more desk or bench time, a would-be mayor or the lonely seek company, a festival lover is keener on the festival; a sitting mayor keeps in touch with the town as keenly as anyone who'd like the job), make a baby or a proposal a little likelier for those who want one, and push a couple's case for a home at town meetings. A dream that comes true is a headline and the best memory there is ("A dream come true: Gaia Haddad was elected mayor of Northwind"); one out of reach is let go quietly (or, for a rivalry, ends in "made peace with Ula"), and a day later they find another. Dream picks use their own dice, so taking one up doesn't reshuffle the town's.
- **Courtship, weddings and funerals** (`src/sim/rites.ts`): two single adults who like each other start courting ("fell in love and are courting"), seek each other out, and after six hours or so the bolder one proposes; the wedding is the next evening at 17:00 in the park (at home if it rains or in winter, and after any festival or rally). Family, housemates, friends and anyone who knows the couple well come; they're partners from the ceremony and move in together afterwards, so babies and a home of their own come after the wedding. A courtship that sours ends in heartbreak, and the grieving don't court. A death with a cemetery to bury in is followed by a funeral there at 10:00 the next morning: everyone who grieves comes, the closest one there speaks ("Mara spoke at Bram's funeral: Bram worked as a carpenter, raised 3 children and lived to 81", drawn only from what's true of their life), and saying goodbye together softens the grief. The inspector shows "courting" or "engaged to", and the director films both.

## What you control

- **Priorities** (Town view, `T`): Housing, Food, Wood & stone, Community, each Low / Normal / High. They weight what town meetings choose and how many people work each job.
- **World events** (Town view): hold a festival, a bumper harvest, send newcomers (even if there's no room, which forces a housing push), call a trader, a drought, a storm, a blizzard, an outbreak.
- **Saving** (Town view): the town autosaves to your browser every game hour and reopens where you left off. Export downloads a save file, Import loads one, New town starts over (click twice). A `?seed=N` link opens a scratch town that is never saved.
- **While you were away** (`src/sim/digest.ts`, `src/ui/away.ts`): come back after a minute or more with the tab hidden or closed, and the town catches up on the time you were gone at the speed it was running (up to one game day, fast-forwarding on screen in about a second), then a card tells you what happened: births, deaths, elections, new buildings, disasters first, then love, coming of age and town meetings, six lines at most, plus anything to worry about right now (people with nowhere to live, winter stores running short). Leaving it running untouched for ten minutes, on a second monitor say, gets the same card when you next touch the mouse or keyboard. A checkbox on the card turns catching up off.
- **Director mode** (`src/sim/director.ts`, `src/ui/director.ts`; `C` or the camera button in the top bar): the side panels step aside and the camera glides between shots on its own, holding each for 9 to 15 seconds: fresh news (it follows whoever a birth, death, election or new love is about), arguments and new friendships as they happen, crowds (a festival or the election rally in the park, birthday parties, the Friday office party), building sites near completion, quiet life (couples on their evening walk, someone at the piano, a grown-up walking the kids to school, a visit to a grave) and now and then the whole town. A caption says who or what and why, in the inspector's words. Headlines and crowds cut in on quieter shots; anything just shown is held back so the camera roams. It starts by itself after two minutes without input (a checkbox on the caption turns that off) and never draws on the town's random numbers, so watching doesn't change what happens.

## Calendar

A season lasts two days and a year eight (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter); the top bar shows the weekday and season ("Tue · Spring 2"), with the year on hover. Ages run on their own faster clock, two years per day, so families turn over within a few in-game years.

## What's simulated

- **Needs:** Energy, Hunger, Hygiene, Social, Fun, Productivity (plus a hidden bladder that drives restroom trips).
- **Utility AI** (`src/sim/ai.ts`): every few minutes each person scores options (work, standup, eat at the office/café/home, cook, coffee, snack, sleep, shower, restroom, chat, ping pong, piano, read, relax, stroll, groceries, fix the coffee machine, party), weighted by needs, schedule, traits and location, with inertia so they don't dither. The inspector shows what they're doing and why.
- **Roles and tasks:** engineers, designers, a writer and a team lead at desks; an office manager at reception; a barista, shopkeeper and librarian at counters. Tasks progress with skill, mood and energy, have due dates, and complete or slip.
- **Traits:** Introvert/Extrovert, Workaholic/Slacker, Foodie, Neat Freak, Early Bird/Night Owl, Cheerful/Grumpy, Competitive, Creative.
- **Social:** conversations go well or turn into arguments based on compatibility, mood and history; people bond while eating, meeting, playing and partying; friendships and rivalries are announced in the feed.
- **Town events:** daily standup, surprise pizza, Friday party, the office coffee machine breaking (an engineer or the office manager fixes it), rain, traffic with pedestrians who wait at the curb.
- **Pathfinding** (`src/sim/nav.ts`): 8-way grid A* that won't cut wall or furniture corners and prefers sidewalks and crosswalks over roads, with a cached connected-regions check so unreachable targets fail instantly.

## Project layout

```
public/assets/kenney/   Kenney CC0 sheets (city, urban, indoors) with their licenses
src/assets/             manifest.ts: every frame by sheet+cell; loader.ts: textures, outfit recolouring, avatars
src/world/              map.ts: mutable tile grid, rooms, buildings, props; town.ts: starting town + wilderness;
                        blueprints.ts / stamp.ts: building templates the townsfolk construct
src/sim/                sim.ts (clock, calendar, step), ai.ts, planner.ts, labor.ts, life.ts, economy.ts, agents.ts,
                        social.ts, events.ts, traffic.ts, nav.ts, save.ts (save/load),
                        defs.ts (needs, goods, roles, traits and memories as data tables)
src/render/             worldView.ts (live ground/walls/props, construction sites), agentsView.ts (people, cars, badges, rain),
                        shading.ts (lawn detail, cast shadows, ambient occlusion), lighting.ts (time-of-day grade, night lights)
src/engine/camera.ts    smoothed camera with viewport-aware clamping
src/ui/                 hud (top), tracker + town panel (left), activity (bottom), inspector (right, tabbed), relationship graph
PRODUCT.md, DESIGN.md   design context: who it's for, tokens, layout and rendering rules for new UI
tests/                  nav + town connectivity, daily routines and events, the self-building town, save/load
```

The sim has no rendering dependencies, so it runs headless in tests (two simulated weeks of a growing town take about 13 seconds).

## Tiles

16×16 pixel tiles rendered at 2× by default with nearest-neighbour scaling. To swap in another 16px pack (LimeZu Modern Interiors, for example), edit `src/assets/manifest.ts`; nothing else references sheet coordinates.
