# Real-World Treasury Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Make the treasury work like a real town's: residents pay taxes, businesses earn from what they sell, the town pays only public jobs and keeps its surplus, outside money comes from the trader and arriving families, and jobs grow with demand.

**Architecture:** `sim.stock` stays the one physical store, because about 40 places read it. Ownership is an accounting rule on top: every good belongs to the business that trades it (`sellerOf`), and each business has a till (`sim.tills`). Sales move money into tills, tills pay their workers, and the town buys materials at market price. New money rules live in a new `src/sim/business.ts`; `src/sim/market.ts` keeps wages, taxes, prices and midnight bookkeeping.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, Vitest, vite-node, PixiJS (UI untouched apart from the Town view).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-growth-and-treasury-design.md` (sections "Treasury: work like a real town" and "Treasury details"). The growth section is a later plan. Borrowing is optional in the spec and is left out here; it can follow once tax income is measured.

**One deliberate reading of the spec, for review:** the spec says "each farm, workshop and shop keeps its own stock". This plan has one business **per trade** (all farms share a till), not one per building. Farmers aren't tied to a farm today, and goods aren't stored per building, so a per-building split would mean rewriting all the `sim.stock` sites and hauling for no difference the player can see in the money. A later plan can split tills by building without changing these rules.

## Global Constraints

- Businesses: `farms` (farmer; food, herbs), `woodyard` (woodcutter; wood, stone, firewood), `workshop` (carpenter, mason, toolmaker; planks, bricks, furniture, tools), `market` (shopkeeper), `cafes` (barista, cook). Medicine is the town's.
- Public jobs, paid by the treasury: builder, laborer, teacher, doctor, librarian. Office roles are paid by outside clients.
- Pensions stay: retirees with under $60 saved get $12 a day. The hardship top-up covers everyone working and tops up to $12 while the treasury keeps $100.
- Rates below are starting values; only Task 10 tunes them: `INCOME_TAX = 0.15`, `SALES_TAX = 0.08`, `PROPERTY_TAX = 3`, `MARKET_MARGIN = 0.2`, `MEAL_MARKUP = 1.5`.
- Performance: `payWage` runs for every worker on every step and must stay O(1). A step stays under about 0.5ms at 200 people on ordinary hours.
- Saves: `SAVE_VERSION` stays 1. New state gets a default in `loadSim`, as existing fields do.
- Determinism: all randomness goes through `sim.rng`. Seed-tuned tests are retuned in Task 10 by changing setup, never by weakening what a test checks.
- Between tasks, run only the test files each task names plus `npm run build`. Long-run solvency tests in `tests/economy.test.ts` may fail until Task 10 tunes the rates, and the rest of the suite may shift with the money.
- Machine load skews timings and can time out workers: run single test files, and long measurements in the background.
- Match the surrounding code: short doc comments on exports, plain-language log lines.
- Every commit message ends with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.
- Commit each task on a new branch `treasury`, created from `treasury-decisions`.
